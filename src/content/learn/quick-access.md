## Command palette

**Ctrl+K** (⌘K if you're on a Mac keyboard) opens a finder that covers the whole app: views, albums, artists, playlists, tracks, subscribed shows, actions, and settings.

Type freely, or narrow with a prefix:

| Prefix | Mode |
|---|---|
| `>` | Actions |
| `@` | Artists |
| `#` | Playlists |
| `/` | Settings |

<!-- shot: palette -->

Picking a result does the obvious thing and closes the palette. An album, artist, or track starts playing right away. A playlist opens in Curate. A show downloads its latest episode and opens the show.

Actions under `>` cover playback (**Play / Pause**, **Next track**, **Toggle shuffle**, **Shuffle all songs**, **Cycle repeat**, **Clear queue**) and **Toggle Zen mode**. Beyond those:

- **Open Tag write-back**, **Open Rip CD**, and **Open Equalizer**
- **Turn equalizer on/off**, and **Equalizer:** followed by the name of each preset you've saved
- **Toggle visualizer**, **Next visualizer**, **Previous visualizer**, and **Visualizer:** followed by an effect name, to jump straight to one
- **Measure missing ReplayGain**, which measures every track that doesn't have gain values yet
- **Run first-run setup again**

Settings under `/` work in place when they can. An on/off setting flips, and a setting with a short list of choices moves to the next one. Anything that needs a number, a slider, a folder, or some text takes you to its row in Settings instead.

The search box in the title bar opens the same palette. You can hide that search box, or hide the tab bar and get around with the palette alone, from Interface settings.

## The Tools menu

The title bar has a **Tools** menu with **Tag write-back**, **Rip CD**, and **Equalizer**, followed by an **Equalizer on** switch and an **Equalizer preset** submenu that shows which curve is live. The Tools tab has the same list: its icon opens the tab, and the small arrow beside it opens the menu. Either way, you can turn the EQ on or switch presets from any tab.

## Quick Menu

On Now Playing, the Quick Menu slides in from the **left** so it never covers the Tunedeck. It holds:

- Favorite playlists
- Recent additions
- Favorite artists

The lists are short and refresh each time you open it. It's a shortcut, not a second library.

## Keyboard

Playback and navigation have a fixed set of shortcuts, and your keyboard's media keys drive Oscine through the OS. You can't rebind them yet. **Help → Keyboard shortcuts** in the app lists them, and **Ctrl+K** is the one to learn first.

The full set, including palette prefixes, visualizer keys, double-click behavior, and media keys, is in [Keyboard & shortcuts](/learn/keyboard).
