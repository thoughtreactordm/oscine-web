Everything here lives in Settings → Theme.

## Mode and theme

**Mode** is **Match system**, **Light**, or **Dark**. Then pick a theme. Each one comes in both light and dark.

- **Oscine:** warm amber on taupe. The house look.
- **Nocturne:** cold verdigris on a blue-shifted gray.
- **High Contrast:** every text weight pushed apart, for bright rooms and cheap panels.

## Reactive color

Turn it on and the accent follows the cover of whatever is playing. It only drives the primary accent, and an accent you set yourself in the token editor still wins.

## Noctalia on Linux

If your Linux desktop runs Noctalia, Oscine can match it. Set **Color source** to **Noctalia desktop** (the other choice is **Oscine theme**). **Set up Noctalia** installs Oscine's template into Noctalia, so every color scheme change on your desktop reaches Oscine. Noctalia supplies the surfaces, text, and supporting colors. The accent still comes from your theme, or from the cover when **Reactive color** is on.

## Token editor

This is where you make Oscine your own: color roles, accents, surfaces, text, borders, type, shape, motion, and the now-playing backdrop. You can seed a ramp from one color, pick a Tailwind palette, or set each step by hand.

<!-- shot: theme-editor -->

Contrast warnings appear on the row that caused them. They never stop you from saving. A deliberately low-contrast look is allowed, and the editor just lets you know.

## Sharing a theme

**Export theme…** saves your look as an `.osctheme` file, and **Import theme…** loads one, both from Settings → Theme. That's how a theme moves to another machine or to a friend. **Export settings…** leaves your theme out, so a theme file is the way to bring it along.
